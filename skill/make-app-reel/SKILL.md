# Make a reel about an app built in Studio

Every reel starts from zero. Look at this app, this person and this build as if no reel had
ever been made before. Do not copy an earlier reel's script, jokes, shots, music, sound
effects, end card or structure; past reels show what quality looks like, never what to make.

## What the reel is for

1. **First: show that building with Studio is easy.** The viewer should finish thinking "I
   could do that, and it would take me five minutes." The prompt the person typed is the star.
2. **Second: show the app.** Make the viewer want it, in one or two clear moments.

Everything else serves those two goals or gets cut.

## What it should feel like

- **Home-grown, not an ad.** A friend showing you something cool they made, the way a good
  TikTok feels. Nobody is selling anything: no "game-changer", no call to action, no
  countdowns, no hard-sell end card, no stock-ad energy.
- **Easy to watch.** Short, to the point, one idea at a time. Aim for 20-40 seconds of
  speaking. If a line doesn't serve goal 1 or 2, cut it.
- **Easy to read and understand.** Every word is heard in full and captioned plainly.
- **Calm sound.** Music is warm and unhurried. Never stressful, tense, chaotic or loud.

## Cut anything that makes building feel hard

The build should look like: an idea, a prompt, a working app. Remove or minimise:

- long setup, configuration, error messages, retries;
- talk of how long it took ("I spent an hour on..."), iterations, fixes, rework;
- process words: "then I had to", "after a lot of back and forth", "it took a few tries";
- anything that reads as technical or as homework.

**Keep the account connection cards.** If the app uses the creator's data, Studio's cards
asking to connect an account (email, Slack, calendar...) are worth showing: they prove how
easy it is to bring real data in, a click or two. Film them from the build chat.

## The flow

### 1. Interview the creator (always, before writing anything)

The creator gives you raw material; you write the story. Don't expect them to be a good
storyteller, and never ask them to come up with the story, the hook or the jokes. Ask for
concrete facts and moments, in plain words, one or two questions at a time, and offer your
own read of the app alongside each so they can react instead of invent.

1. **What's the most interesting thing about this app?** The thing a stranger would say
   "wait, it does that?" about.
2. **What was life like before it?** The annoyance, the mess, the thing they kept putting
   off. This is the story's starting point.
3. **What made you finally build it?** The trigger moment, if there was one.
4. **What was the most fun or the easiest part of building it?** The heart of the reel.
   Push for the specific moment: the one sentence they typed, the first time it worked.
5. **What did you type to start it?** Find the original prompt in the build chat (search
   this workspace's past chats) and show it to them; the real words beat a paraphrase.
6. **What surprised you?** Something it did that they didn't expect, or did better.
7. **What's different now?** One concrete after-picture, not a feature list.

Follow up on anything vivid. Specific details (a number, a name of a thing, a feeling, an
exact phrase) are what make a story land; generic answers make generic reels.

### 2. Find the story (from first principles)

Write down for this app:

- **The one thing a viewer must remember** (one sentence).
- **The proof of ease:** the shortest true path from idea to working app, usually the
  prompt itself on screen.
- **The app's best visual moment:** what it looks like when it does its main thing. Choose
  something that moves or changes on screen, and that reads at phone size in one second.

Then build a real plot. A list of facts is not a story. Use a proven narrative structure,
chosen for this app, for example:

- **Problem, attempt, payoff:** a relatable frustration, one prompt to Studio, it works.
- **Setup and twist:** a plain premise, then a surprising turn (what it found, what it did).
- **Before and after:** how it was, the moment it changed, how it is now.
- **Small mystery:** open on a curious result, then rewind to how one prompt made it.

Whatever the structure, the story needs:

- **A hook in the first two seconds:** a tension, question or surprise the viewer wants
  resolved. Usually spoken over the creator's face and the Studio icon; the app waits.
- **A protagonist with a want:** the creator, wanting something any viewer has wanted.
- **A turn:** the moment they asked Studio, told simply. This is where ease is proven.
- **A payoff:** the app doing its thing, the hook's question answered.
- **A button:** a last line that lands the feeling and closes the loop on the hook.

Every beat should cause the next one ("so", "but", "then it"), not just follow it.
Cut any beat the story can lose.

### 3. Offer tones, let the creator choose

Read the app and offer 2-3 tones that genuinely fit it, with a one-line sample of how the
hook would sound in each, and recommend one. Fit the tone to the app, not to a formula:

- **A serious app** (health, money, work, care) can have a serious, sincere, quietly
  confident reel.
- **A creative app** can be artsy: visual, poetic, atmospheric, slower.
- **A playful or relatable app** can lean into dry humour, deadpan, self-deprecation or
  wordplay.
- Also consider: warm and personal, curious and nerdy, calm and matter-of-fact.

Only offer humour or puns where they truly suit the use case, and never force a joke.
Not every reel should be funny; trying too hard is worse than being plain. Also check it
against how the creator naturally talks: they have to say these lines.

Once the creator picks, commit to that tone everywhere: word choice, rhythm, pacing, the
music's mood, the captions' feel and the end card. The aim is a reel with a voice of its
own, not a house style.

### 4. Write the script

- Write it yourself, fully, in the chosen tone and structure. Short spoken lines, in words
  the creator would use. Every line true.
- Propose 2-3 directions (different hooks or structures, not variations of one), recommend
  one, and say why it serves goal 1 then goal 2.
- Lead with the story's tension; the prompt and its ease are the turn; the app arrives as
  the payoff.
- End on the button, not a pitch. Then a quiet end card: app name and icon, a short
  tagline, "Made with Studio".
- For each line, say what's on screen. Read it aloud at speaking pace and give the length.
- Iterate until the creator would actually say it. Save the final script as a document.

### 5. Make the screen footage (made-up data only)

- **Never put the creator's real data on screen.** Run a private copy of the app on
  believable made-up records. For the build chat, measure where real data (names,
  messages, amounts, numbers) starts and make the recording refuse to scroll past it.
- Record frame by frame at 1080x1920 so motion is smooth and text sharp.
- Film the original prompt from the build chat; it's the most important shot.
- Show sample frames before calling footage done.

### 6. The creator films one take

Phone upright, eye level, window light, one take straight to camera, keep going through
stumbles, a second of silence at the start and end. They attach the file to the chat.

### 7. Edit

**Words are sacred.**
- Transcribe with word timings. Cut silences and "um"s only in the gaps *between* words,
  never inside a word. Keep at least ~0.1 s of air before each word starts and ~0.15 s
  after it ends, so first and last syllables survive.
- After cutting, re-transcribe the cut audio and compare it word for word with the
  original. Any missing or clipped word is a defect; fix it before going on. Also listen
  for trailing sounds that a transcript can't show by checking the level just before and
  after each cut: it should be at room-tone, not mid-word.
- Don't cut whole lines the creator didn't ask to cut, except ones that make building
  look hard (see above); flag those cuts to them.

**Captions stay put.**
- One fixed position for the whole reel (a consistent spot in the lower-middle, clear of
  the platform's bottom bar). Never move, jump or animate them between shots.
- 2-5 words at a time, plain and large, readable over a face, white screens and dark
  screens (a solid box with a thin contrasting edge works). Highlighting the current word
  is fine; bouncing, scaling or recolouring whole lines isn't.
- Captions match what was said, spelled right (check names like "Studio" and the app name).

**Picture.**
- Gentle, not frantic. Jump cuts are fine where silences were removed; a slight zoom
  change hides them. Use punch-ins sparingly, only where a line deserves one.
- App and chat footage plays over the creator's voice, zoomed to the part that matters,
  with their face in a small corner box so the viewer stays with the person.
- iPhone video is often HDR; convert it so skin isn't blown out.

**Sound: original every time.**
- Compose new music for this reel. Pick a mood from the app and the creator's tone, then
  write it fresh: new chords, tempo, instruments and melody. Never reuse a loop or sound
  from an earlier reel. Synthesising it yourself (e.g. in numpy) keeps it royalty-free.
- Calm and DIY: soft, warm, unhurried (roughly 70-95 BPM), simple. No risers, drops,
  alarms, heavy bass hits or busy drums. Music sits well under the voice (about 16 dB).
- Sound effects are optional and few: soft and organic (a light key click, a quiet pop).
  Skip any effect that's jarring or cartoonish, and never put one over a word.
- Voice at normal social loudness (about -14 LUFS), with a limiter on the final mix.

### 8. Review before showing

Watch it as a stranger scrolling past would, then check:

- Does the first two seconds make you want to keep watching?
- Would a viewer come away thinking building this was easy? Is anything left that feels
  hard, long or process-heavy?
- Is the app's best moment clear?
- Is it a story, not a list? Does the hook's question get answered, and does the last
  line close the loop?
- Does the tone hold all the way through, and does it suit this app? Any joke that feels
  forced goes.
- Is every word heard in full? (Compare transcripts.) Do captions stay in one place?
- Does the music stay calm throughout? Does anything feel like an ad?
- Is it as short as it can be?

You can't hear: check sound by measuring levels and drawing a spectrogram, and tell the
creator to listen with sound on. Check picture with a contact sheet of frames across the
whole reel. Then show it to the creator and ask what to change.
